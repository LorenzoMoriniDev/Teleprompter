Why the Text Has to Be Mirrored

In a beamsplitter rig the screen sits under, or above, a sheet of half-silvered glass angled at 45°, and you read its reflection in that glass while the camera shoots straight through it. The camera sees you looking down the lens; you see the script.

A reflection reverses left and right. So the text that is correct on the screen reads backwards in the glass, and the fix is to reverse it on the screen first: two reversals cancel out, and what you read through the glass is the right way round.

Mirroring is only for rigs where you read a reflection. If the screen is a monitor or a tablet you look at directly, leave it off.

Which Way to Flip

Which axis you need depends on how the screen is mounted.

  • Screen flat, facing up, glass above it: flip left to right. The usual tablet-in-a-hood setup.
  • Screen above the glass, facing down: flip left to right and top to bottom. Hanging the screen upside down has already reversed the vertical, so the vertical flip puts it back.
  • Screen rotated 90° in the hood: try one axis, look through the glass, try the other.

Turn mirroring on and look through the glass from where you will be standing, then keep whichever setting reads correctly.

Setting Up the Rig

  1. Mount the glass in front of the lens at 45°, with the hood blocking stray light from behind it.
  2. Position the screen so the glass picks it up: flat under the glass, or inverted above it.
  3. Turn the screen's brightness up. Beamsplitter glass passes most of the light to the camera and reflects only a fraction back to you, so a dim screen is unreadable in the glass.
  4. Paste the script, set the pace in words per minute or as a total duration, and turn on mirroring.
  5. Check through the glass from where you will actually be standing, and set the font size and text width from there, not from the desk.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does teleprompter text need to be mirrored?

In a beamsplitter rig you read the screen as a reflection in a sheet of glass, and a reflection reverses left and right. The text has to be flipped on the screen so that the reflection you read is the right way round.

Which way should I mirror the text?

It depends on how the screen is mounted. A screen lying flat and facing up, under the glass, needs a left-to-right flip. A screen mounted above the glass and facing down needs a top-to-bottom flip as well, because hanging it upside down already reverses the vertical. The quickest check is to turn mirroring on and look through the glass, keeping whichever setting reads correctly.

Do I need a mirrored teleprompter without a beamsplitter?

No. If you are reading the screen directly, from a second monitor or a tablet under the lens, the text should not be mirrored. Mirroring is only for rigs where you read a reflection.

Is there a free mirrored teleprompter?

Yes. The teleprompter on this site mirrors left to right and top to bottom, free, with no account and no install. Only the text is flipped: the controls sit on top of it and stay readable.
